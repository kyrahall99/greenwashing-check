# Environmental marketing rules reference

This is a plain-language summary of the U.S. FTC Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260)
and the EU Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (EU 2024/825).
Each rule has an ID. Cite rule IDs exactly as written here.

## General principles

- **GG-260.3a Qualifications must be clear and close.** Any qualifying language must
  be clear, prominent, and placed next to the claim it qualifies.
- **GG-260.3b Product vs. package.** A claim must say whether it refers to the
  product, the package, or a part of either, unless that is obvious from context.
- **GG-260.3c No overstatement.** A claim must not overstate a benefit, expressly or
  by implication (for example "50% more recycled content" when the increase is 2% to 3%).
- **GG-260.3d Comparisons must be clear.** Comparative claims ("greener", "less
  waste") must say what they compare against and must be substantiated.
- **GG-260.2 Substantiation.** The marketer must have competent and reliable
  evidence, often scientific, for every reasonable interpretation of the claim
  before making it.

## Specific claims

- **GG-260.4 General environmental benefit.** Unqualified general claims such as
  "eco-friendly", "green", "environmentally friendly", "earth-friendly", or
  "sustainable" are almost impossible to substantiate and should not be made.
  They must be qualified with specific, limited benefits, and the qualification
  must not imply other benefits that are not true.
- **GG-260.5 Carbon offsets.** Offset claims need competent and reliable
  scientific and accounting methods, must not double-count, and must disclose
  when the emission reduction will not happen for two years or more. Offsets
  must not be for reductions that were already required by law.
- **GG-260.6 Certifications and seals.** A seal or certification is an
  endorsement. It must disclose any material connection to the certifier and
  must be qualified to say what specific benefit it certifies. A seal created by
  the seller itself is deceptive if it implies third-party certification.
- **GG-260.7 Compostable.** Requires evidence that all materials break down into
  usable compost in a safe and timely manner (about the same time as other
  compost material). If not compostable at home, the claim must say so, and must
  qualify if municipal or industrial facilities are not available to a
  substantial majority of consumers where the product is sold.
- **GG-260.8 Degradable / biodegradable.** Unqualified "degradable" or
  "biodegradable" claims require evidence that the entire item completely breaks
  down and returns to nature within a reasonably short time (one year) after
  customary disposal. Items customarily landfilled, incinerated, or recycled
  should not carry unqualified degradable claims.
- **GG-260.9 Free-of.** "Free of X" is deceptive if the product contains X
  (beyond trace or background levels), if it contains substances with a similar
  risk, or if X was never associated with that product category.
- **GG-260.10 Non-toxic.** "Non-toxic" implies safety for both people and the
  environment and needs evidence for both.
- **GG-260.11 Ozone-safe / ozone-friendly.** Deceptive if the product contains
  any ozone-depleting substance.
- **GG-260.12 Recyclable.** May be claimed without qualification only if
  recycling facilities are available to a substantial majority (60%) of
  consumers or communities where the item is sold. Otherwise it must be qualified
  ("may not be recyclable in your area"). Components that are not recyclable
  must be disclosed. A recycling symbol alone is a recyclable claim.
- **GG-260.13 Recycled content.** Only material diverted from the waste stream
  counts. Partial content must be stated as a percentage or amount.
- **GG-260.14 Refillable.** Requires a system for refilling the package, provided
  or reasonably available.
- **GG-260.15 Renewable energy.** "Made with renewable energy" must specify the
  source and the share if not all significant manufacturing used renewable energy.
  It is deceptive if the seller sold the renewable energy certificates for that
  power. Avoid unqualified claims if power came from fossil sources offset by RECs
  without disclosure.
- **GG-260.16 Renewable materials.** Should say what the material is, how it is
  sourced, and why it is renewable; qualify if not 100% renewable (excluding
  minor fasteners).
- **GG-260.17 Source reduction.** "Less waste" claims must be qualified with the
  amount and the basis of comparison.

## Textile and fiber claims (FTC enforcement practice)

- **FTC-TEX Bamboo rayon.** Fabric made from rayon or viscose processed from
  bamboo must be labeled "rayon" or "viscose" (for example "rayon made from
  bamboo"). Calling it "bamboo" and touting bamboo's natural benefits (grown
  without pesticides, naturally antimicrobial) is deceptive, because the chemical
  process uses toxic chemicals and the fiber keeps none of bamboo's properties.

## EU rules (Directive 2024/825, applies from 27 September 2026)

- **EU-GENERIC Generic claims banned.** Generic environmental claims ("eco",
  "green", "nature's friend", "climate friendly", "environmentally friendly",
  "biodegradable", "eco-conscious") are banned in the EU unless the business can
  show recognised excellent environmental performance relevant to the claim.
- **EU-OFFSET Offset-based neutrality banned.** Claims that a product has a
  neutral, reduced, or positive impact on the environment in terms of greenhouse
  gas emissions ("carbon neutral", "climate neutral", "CO2 compensated") based on
  offsetting are banned.
- **EU-LABEL Self-made sustainability labels banned.** Sustainability labels
  must be based on a certification scheme or set by public authorities.
- **EU-WHOLE Whole vs. part.** A claim about the entire product or business is
  banned when it is only true of one aspect.
- **EU-FUTURE Future performance claims.** Claims about future environmental
  performance ("net zero by 2030") need a clear, verifiable implementation plan
  with targets and independent monitoring.
